Museum Lucis

Museum
Graz, Austria
2021
Completed

This digital arts museum in Graz, Austria is built on a central paradox: a building dedicated to light is housed within a heavy, almost windowless concrete shell. The museum’s brutalist-inspired form stands as a bold, geometric mass in the city, its facade revealing little of the glowing world contained inside.

This deliberate contrast is key to the visitor’s experience. The monolithic exterior functions as a threshold, preparing visitors for the immersive galleries within. By eliminating external light and distraction, the architecture focuses all attention on the interactive digital exhibits. The building becomes an essential part of the art, a dark chamber that makes the experience of light the main event.

Country Austria

Architecture Rojdi Alici

“A dark chamber that makes the experience of light the main event.”

The museum is built on a central paradox: a building dedicated to light housed within a heavy, almost windowless concrete shell. This tension between interior and exterior defines every design decision, from the massing of the facade to the sequencing of galleries within. The visitor moves from darkness into light, a spatial narrative that mirrors the art itself.

The brutalist shell functions as a threshold, a deliberate barrier between the noise of the city and the contemplative world inside. By eliminating external light and distraction, the monolithic exterior prepares visitors for the immersive galleries within. The building itself becomes an instrument of perception.
