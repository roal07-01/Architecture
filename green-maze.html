Green Maze

Residential Module
Graz, Austria
2022
Completed

This project tackles a key question in shared living: How can six different people have privacy without being closed off by solid walls? It offers a solution that isn’t just a choice between separate rooms or one big open space, but a comfortable middle ground that helps people live together well.

The answer is in the design of the space itself, inspired by Japanese architects like Sou Fujimoto. The inside of the module is one large area with many different floor levels, all set at various heights. Each level is designed for a specific purpose, like a quiet corner for reading or a small garden. Short, open stairs connect all these platforms, making it easy to move around.

This layout lets each resident have their own level, which feels like their own personal spot. Privacy comes from being on a different height, not from being behind a wall. Even though everyone has their own zone, they can still see across the open space and feel part of the larger home. The design feels both separated and connected, helping build a community where people can have their own space and choose when to interact.

Country Austria
Client Academic
Year 2022
Architecture Rojdi Alici

“Privacy through elevation, not enclosure. A home you discover vertically.”

The split-level concept transforms a single volume into a landscape of distinct zones. Instead of dividing the space with walls, changes in floor height create natural boundaries between activities. Each platform becomes a room without edges, open to the whole yet unmistakably its own. The result is a spatial experience that encourages both solitude and togetherness.

The plan reveals the influence of Sou Fujimoto’s spatial philosophy, treating architecture as a series of gradients rather than hard separations. Overlapping platforms and staggered stairs allow sightlines to travel freely, while the varying heights give each zone a sense of enclosure without ever closing it off. The floor plan reads less like a conventional layout and more like a topographic map of shared living.
